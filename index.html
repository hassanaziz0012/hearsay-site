Phone hears.PC types. Phone hears.PC types.

Press Ctrl+Alt+Space, talk to Rambler on your Android phone, press again: the cleaned-up text is pasted where you're typing.

Fast enough to forget it's there

Hearsay wakes the phone, opens a text field and presses Gboard's mic key for you. Measured on a Pixel 9: listening 1.9 seconds after the first press, from a dozing phone, and the text pasted 0.7 to 1.0 seconds after the second.

Hearsay finds Gboard's mic key by its internal ID, so a Gboard update can stop dictation from starting until Hearsay catches up.

Questions

Is Hearsay made by Google?

No. Rambler is Google's voice typing in Gboard. Hearsay is an independent, open-source project that drives it from your computer, and isn't affiliated with Google.

Does Hearsay hear me?

No. Gboard does the listening, on the phone. Hearsay presses its mic key, waits for the finished text and sends it to your computer.

What goes over the network?

The start and stop signals and the finished text, over your own Wi-Fi, encrypted, between the phone and the computer you paired it with. Nothing goes through a server.

Can the phone stay locked?

Yes. Leave it on the desk, locked if you like. You talk to it; you don't need to touch it.

Why isn't it on the Play Store?

Not yet. You install the app from the release page, which means turning Play Protect off for the install and allowing a restricted setting for the key presser. The setup window walks you through both.

Dictation stopped starting. Now what?

Gboard probably moved its mic key. Run hearsay diagnose and attach the file to an issue. It holds Gboard's key names and positions, never anything you said.
